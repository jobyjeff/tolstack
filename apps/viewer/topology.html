How to read the map and the grid

  • A box is a part, a pin on its edge is a feature where it meets another part. A line inside a box is one of the part's dimensions, between the two features it measures.
  • A line between two pins is an interface, drawn with the joint's symbol where it crosses: a cup and ball for a ball joint, a circle and dot for a pin in a bore, the same with a turning arrow for a rotary joint, a ring of dots for a fastener pattern, a bar with a double arrow for a slide, two stops for a clearance, two plates for a seat. Several constraints of one joint run side by side.
  • A dashed field is a rigid body, each in its own ink, and an outline round a part and the parts inside it is an assembly.
  • The grid beside the map holds one row per dimension, each part's rows together under its bracket, a body's parts under the body's. Point at anything on either and the same thing lights on both, in the tree and in the 3D pane.
  • The wheel zooms the map and a drag pans it; Fit, or the 0 key, shows the whole map again.
  • A gap is a row described "across a clearance" — its two interfaces share no part. A DERIVED gap carries no value at all: it is the quantity a study computes, and a study that puts it in its selection is refused.
  • A study that reaches a branch point with two unconsumed selected edges raises BranchAmbiguity and names both. Which parallel path binds is a mechanics question this tool does not answer and must not learn to — see docs/DAG_TOPOLOGY.md, "Not a solver".
  • Every study says whether it passes, and by how much. Its answer is the last block of the grid, read like an invoice: a totals section under the members it totals, then one verdict row per check — the verdict word, what the check is about, the signed margin, and the limit it is judged against. A study nobody has yet written a criterion for says exactly that rather than staying blank. Where the verdict is outlined in amber, a dimension the joint needs is missing from the chain — the verdict is then true of the model and not of the hardware, and what is missing is the first row of the summary underneath.
  • A loud badge is a number you should not trust yet. unverified means nothing readable stands behind it; no tolerance recorded means the value is sourced but its plus/minus is not, so every spread it feeds is a lower bound. A badge appears on the row that earned it; the study that sums it lists the same thing as one line of its summary, with the reason it was written down one click deeper. What's missing, at the foot of the summary, is the same story for the whole assembly, with what would close each gap.
  • Selecting a study highlights its chain, numbers each row with its place in the sum, prints each edge's own signed and scaled contribution, and adds the rolled-up totals as rows of the same grid, in the same columns. Every one of those numbers came out of tolerance_stack.topology.summarize() → fold(). Nothing on this page is added up in JavaScript.
