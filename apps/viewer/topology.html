How to read the rails

  • One row per graph element, nodes and edges interleaved: a dot is an interface, a bar is the dimension between two of them. Every id in the document has exactly one row and one mark, and the two share a y by construction.
  • A column is a branch, not a part. The walk is depth-first from the document's first node; a branch keeps its column until it rejoins or ends, which is why a fork fans out sideways and a grounded loop closes with a dashed curve back up to the interface it lands on.
  • A bar's colour is its citation's confidence — the same green / amber / UNTRACED / NO CITATION vocabulary the elements table uses. The rails themselves are neutral on purpose: those four colours are reserved for provenance here, so lane identity is carried by position and by the part named on the row.
  • A dashed bar is a gap — its two interfaces share no part, so it crosses between lanes and has no rail of its own. A DERIVED gap carries no value at all: it is the quantity a study computes, and a study that puts it in its selection is refused.
  • ⑂ marks a branch point. A study reaching one with two unconsumed selected edges raises BranchAmbiguity and names both. Which parallel path binds is a mechanics question this tool does not answer and must not learn to — see docs/DAG_TOPOLOGY.md, "Not a solver".
  • Selecting a study highlights its chain, numbers each row with its place in the sum, prints each edge's own signed and scaled contribution, and puts the totals in the footer below. Every one of those numbers came out of tolerance_stack.topology.summarize() → fold(). Nothing on this page is added up in JavaScript.
